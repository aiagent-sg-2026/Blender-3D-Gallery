# Planar-cap normal prevention for this asset pack

Bug pattern: a closed tapered rod has valid topology and unit normals, yet its planar caps look rounded and its smooth side develops a pinched highlight.

Where it appeared: the two original billiards cues, generated by the same cone construction. Cue02 remained untouched until its sequential QA turn; this is a repeated construction defect, not a regression of the accepted Cue01 fix.

Root cause: every cone face was smooth and cap boundaries were unmarked. End-cap normals influenced smooth-side shading. On the long butt, the measured source side normal had an absolute axial component near .674 instead of the taper's .01165.

Failed previous approach: the controlled Blender5.1.2 comparison made caps flat without marking their boundaries sharp. That corrected cap faces but left the side highlight pinched in Cycles. No such incomplete fix was published.

Correct fix: `models/eight-ball-billiards/cue_shading.py` owns the source-mesh operation: identify two ngon caps, make them flat, mark only their boundary edges sharp, and keep the side faces smooth. Generator and revision-guarded migration wrappers use that same function. Do not move vertices, change roughness, or blanket-modify unreviewed rods to conceal this shading defect. Keep the helper beside the generator when copying its source folder.

Regression check: `tests/helpers/cue-normal-checks.mjs` compares exported vertex normals with actual oriented triangle normals in both standalone and assembled GLBs. The two straight cues share world X as their known axis. Caps must align above .9999; side alignment exceeds .98, derived from cos(pi/16)=.980785 for the coarsest reviewed side ring. The short tip is wider than it is long: do not infer its axis from the longest bounding-box dimension. For differently oriented geometry, establish the actual primitive axis before reusing this check.

Verification evidence: Cue01 was accepted in main13ef9c10, required Actions43637504290 and production SHA932029a3c25f49d21c2f31547cecb471196f39470555ad2035795174c58a5046. The shared migration reproduced those accepted exports byte-for-byte from an isolated original source copy. Cue02 independently reproduced the same defect and passed12 targeted checks and625 initial full unit checks after its local fix; its remote/live acceptance remains governed by its individual release report, not this lesson.

How to detect next time: inspect cap and side highlights in matched close-ups, then inspect source flags and exported normals. A zero-error glTF Validator result establishes format validity, not shading correctness. Re-import and render the real export; compare source and export independently.

Export preservation: corrected normals can reorder vertex/index buffers. Compare canonical oriented triangles including UVs to establish geometry preservation, and compare every unrelated mesh payload and sibling export. UV/hard-normal seam vertices may duplicate positions; any welding used for topology inspection stays in a disposable analysis mesh and must not rewrite the saved model.

Skill updated or candidate created: keep this prevention rule in project documentation and shared project code. A bounded local/KB Skill search found no confident cap-normal procedure; general product-asset QA and coding-regression routes did not own this specific source convention. No global Skill or user memory was modified.

Do not repeat: do not accept unit-length normals alone, hide the highlight by changing materials, treat a loader/Validator PASS as complete visual QA, infer a short part's axis from its bounding box, or repair an unreviewed next model while applying the shared rule.
